**The AI Engineer World's Fair has one word on its mind: loops**

Day two of AIEWF was essentially a conference-wide argument for the same idea: the future of AI engineering is agents running in persistent, self-repeating cycles rather than single-shot question-and-answer exchanges. AIEWF cofounder Shawn Wang opened with a talk called "Loopcraft: The Art of Stacking Loops," tracing the arc from chat to tools to what he called "automations and cron jobs." OpenAI's Alexander Embiricos put it plainly: connect an agent to why work needs to happen, not just what the work is, and the agent can start much more on its own. Connect it to review and deploy afterward, and it can land much more too.

Why it matters: The companies whose engineers understand how to design these loops well will ship dramatically faster than those still thinking in single prompts. This is the actual skill gap opening up in software teams right now, and most job descriptions don't mention it yet.

**Open-source AI models might be Google's secret weapon**

Madhu Guru posted a counterintuitive argument: the proliferation of strong open-weight models like GLM doesn't hurt Google, it helps them. His logic is that enterprises wanting to fine-tune open models still need managed infrastructure with reliability, security, and support. Google Cloud provides that, and Google also owns a significant chunk of the underlying compute stack.

Why it matters: If this plays out, the companies building open models are doing Google's customer acquisition for free. Every enterprise that starts experimenting with open-weight fine-tuning eventually hits the question of where to run it at scale. Google has a ready answer.
