**Every frontier AI lab is building the same thing, and it might matter sooner than you think**

FlightVC's Matt Turck flagged a sobering framing: every major AI lab right now is working on recursive self-improvement, meaning AI systems that build and improve other AI systems. He pointed to Anthropic researcher Ryan Greenblatt's estimate that this dynamic could produce an AI that meaningfully outpaces human control as early as late 2028 or 2029.

Why it matters: 2028 is not a distant abstraction. It's two product cycles away. If you're making 5-year infrastructure bets, hiring plans, or strategic roadmaps right now, this is the timeline your risk assessment probably isn't pricing in. Whether you believe Greenblatt's estimate or not, the fact that Anthropic's own researchers are saying it out loud is worth sitting with.

**Peter Yang's bot idea is funnier because it's actually useful**

Product leader Peter Yang floated the concept of a bot that handles the errands your spouse asks you to do, noting that the name "errands your spouse wants you to run but you delegated to a bot instead" is simply too long. It's a joke, but only partially.

Why it matters: The most interesting AI agent category right now might not be enterprise workflow automation. It's the small, unglamorous personal delegation tasks that people actually forget to do. Someone is going to build this seriously, and it'll be enormous.
