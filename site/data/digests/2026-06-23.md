**Sakana's new model quietly delegates your work to whoever does it best**

Box CEO Aaron Levie flagged a new release from Sakana called Fugu: a single AI you call with one API, which then figures out which underlying model is best for your specific task, routes the work there, verifies the result, and hands it back to you. No multi-agent setup to configure, no model-selection logic to write yourself. Fugu handles orchestration invisibly.

Why it matters: Every company building AI products right now is managing a growing stack of specialized models. Your team is writing glue code to route tasks between them. Fugu is a bet that this routing layer becomes a product, not something you build yourself. If it works reliably, the companies currently selling you "choose the right model for each task" consulting are in trouble.

**OpenAI's Codex team is asking the public what's broken**

Thibault Sottiaux, who works on the Codex App at OpenAI, posted a direct question to his followers: what's not delightful? The post got 3,380 replies. That's not a small feedback thread.

Why it matters: Yesterday we covered Sottiaux's hint that current Codex models are "okayish at front-end" with better ones coming. Today he's collecting a public bug list. The combination tells you something: OpenAI is close enough to confident about the next generation that they're already cataloging what needs fixing when it ships. If you've been holding off on AI coding tools because the UI output felt off, now is a good time to say so.

**Vercel optimized everything, then wrote it all down**

Vercel CEO Guillermo Rauch posted that his team went through a recent project and scrutinized every frame: painting, layout, WebGPU shaders, blocking scripts. The phrase he used was "everything the light touches." More usefully, he said the lessons are going into Vercel's public documentation.

Why it matters: Performance deep-dives that get published as guides are worth more than the original project. If you're shipping web apps with AI-generated front-ends (and your output quality is still limited, as yesterday's Codex story noted), Vercel's coming documentation update could be a practical cheat sheet for making that output not feel slow.

**Google DeepMind published a security framework for AI agents**

Google DeepMind released a post outlining what they call an AI Control Roadmap for securing internal systems that use AI agents. The approach layers traditional security safeguards with real-time monitoring. The post is from June 16, just inside the window.

Why it matters: Most companies deploying AI agents are doing so with the same security assumptions they use for regular software. An agent that can browse, write code, and call APIs is a much larger attack surface. DeepMind publishing their internal framework gives security teams something concrete to pressure-test their own setups against.

https://deepmind.google/blog/securing-the-future-of-ai-agents/
