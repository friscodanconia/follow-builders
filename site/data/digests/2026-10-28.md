**AI Builders Digest — October 28, 2026**

Two stories today share the same underlying question: how much of the corporate world is actually ready for AI to matter? Replit's CEO is painting a vision of self-driving companies. Box's CEO is pointing at a number that suggests most big companies are still barely dipping a toe in.

---

**Replit CEO Amjad Masad thinks the org chart itself is going away**

Masad published a framework he's calling "the Agent Habitat," arguing that AI agents are about to do to company structure what the spreadsheet did to accounting departments. The thesis: hierarchies of humans coordinating through bureaucracy are a centuries-old workaround for limited compute and communication. Agents don't have those limits. Replit is building infrastructure for what comes after.

Why it matters: Every operations leader and middle manager should read this carefully, because Masad is not talking about AI tools that help your team work faster. He's describing a world where the team itself is reconstituted around a much smaller group of humans making judgment calls while agents handle execution. Whether that's five years away or fifteen, the planning assumption has changed.

https://x.com/amasad/status/2114760898225299721

**Aaron Levie has a number that explains why enterprise AI is still early**

Box CEO Aaron Levie flagged a striking stat: the average Fortune 500 customer of Anthropic spends less than 0.1% of their total expenses on Anthropic. Levie's point is that even accounting for falling token prices, there's nothing stopping that figure from reaching 1-5% of spend, which would still be a fraction of what startups are paying. The gap between frontier users and large enterprise buyers is enormous.

Why it matters: If your company considers itself an AI-forward enterprise because it has a ChatGPT Enterprise license and a few pilot projects, this stat is about you. The companies that figure out where in the 1-5% range to land, and what they get for it, are going to pull away from the ones still debating whether to start.

https://x.com/levie/status/2114761816199098587

**Peter Yang on what comes after the attention economy**

Product builder Peter Yang posted a short prediction: in the near future, people will stop reading or browsing online and just tag an agent to do it for them. He cited tagging-bot behavior as an early signal of this.

Why it matters: If Yang is right, the entire business model of media, newsletters, and content platforms built around human eyeballs needs rethinking. You don't monetize an agent's attention the same way you monetize a person's. The ad industry quietly noticed this problem two years ago and has not solved it.

https://x.com/petergyang/status/2114927195332460940

**Google Beam is now in 20 military bases for deployed service members**

Josh Woodward, who leads Google Labs, announced a partnership with the U.S. Army and the USO to bring Google Beam, Google's 3D video calling technology, to 20 locations where deployed service members can have lifelike video calls with their families.

Why it matters: This is a real-world stress test for Beam in conditions where latency, bandwidth, and emotional stakes are all high. Consumer tech partnerships with the military tend to generate a lot of visibility. If the calls hold up, expect Google to lean on this footage for a long time.
