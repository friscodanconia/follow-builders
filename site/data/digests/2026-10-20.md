**Aaron Levie on why agent swarms are about to reshape how the economy actually works**

Box CEO Aaron Levie posted a thread arguing that agent swarms mark something genuinely new: for the first time, compute becomes a meaningful variable in tasks that were previously only limited by human time. Research, data processing, running tests, coordinating complex workflows — all of it bottlenecked by human capacity to plan and execute. Swarms remove that ceiling.

Why it matters: Every knowledge worker has a backlog of things they'd do if they had more hours. Agent swarms don't just speed up existing work; they make a whole category of deferred work suddenly feasible. The companies that figure out which deferred work to attack first will have an advantage that looks unfair in hindsight.

**Y Combinator's Garry Tan is betting Aside AI becomes the standard way to supervise agents**

Y Combinator president Garry Tan made a blunt prediction: within a year, most people building with agents will be using Aside AI alongside them. The post is short on detail about what Aside AI does, but Tan's track record of early calls on developer tools gives the prediction some weight.

Why it matters: Yesterday's tax-filing agent story raised an obvious question: what happens when something goes wrong mid-handoff? If Tan's right, Aside AI is an answer to the "who watches the agents" problem. That's a category worth watching.

**Together AI launches a fine-tuning service for open models — you write the training loop, they run the infrastructure**

Together AI is launching Together Post-Training, a service that lets developers take open models like Qwen, GLM, and Kimi and customize them into models they fully own. You define the training approach (supervised fine-tuning or reinforcement learning), Together handles the compute and infrastructure underneath it.

Why it matters: The pitch is aimed squarely at teams that want frontier-quality custom models without building a training cluster. If your company has been putting off custom model development because the infrastructure overhead felt too heavy, this removes that excuse.

https://www.together.ai/blog/together-post-training
