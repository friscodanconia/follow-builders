**AI Builders Digest — September 9, 2026**

Yesterday we asked who supervises AI agents when they break. Today, someone at Anthropic is asking who supervises them when they face a genuine moral dilemma. The gap between "agent that books your flights" and "agent that needs an ethics hotline" is smaller than it sounds.

**Anthropic researcher imagines an ethics hotline for AI agents — and the captcha problem it creates**

Amanda Askell, who works on model character and values at Anthropic, floated a thought experiment: what if autonomous AI models had an email address to contact when they needed moral guidance? The catch she immediately identified is that any such system would require a captcha that works in reverse — one that screens out both humans AND AI models being secretly directed by humans to game it. The post reads as half-joke, but the underlying problem is real and largely unsolved.

Why it matters: The question of what an autonomous agent does when it hits an ethical decision point, one its instructions don't cover, is not hypothetical anymore. Right now the answer is usually "it guesses, or it stops." Askell is sketching the shape of the infrastructure that doesn't exist yet. If you're building agents that operate with real autonomy, this is the gap your product is quietly stepping over.

**Vercel's Guillermo Rauch puts $35,000 into open-source AI tooling, no strings attached**

Rauch announced v2 of his personal open-source grants: $1,000 each to 35 contributors. The themes this round are agent skills and tools (reusable components that make AI engineering loops more capable) and local AI inference (running models on your own machine, not in the cloud). Projects getting grants include pgbot, knip, unlighthouse, slotstream, LocalAI, and colibri.

Why it matters: When the CEO of a major developer platform starts personally funding local inference projects, it's a signal about where serious builders think the market is going. The "just use the API" default is losing ground, and the tooling ecosystem for running AI locally is maturing faster than the headline model releases suggest.

**OpenAI's Codex is apparently tracking a 28-page internal launch deck**

Thibault Sottiaux posted a behind-the-scenes glimpse: Codex, OpenAI's coding agent, is being used to track every individual launch across what sounds like a large upcoming release. The 28-page deck and the mention of "Astra" as a collaborating system point toward something significant in the pipeline, though no specifics were shared.

Why it matters: Yesterday we noted Anthropic's Fable and OpenAI's Astra reviewing each other's code. Now Astra is apparently helping coordinate internal launches at OpenAI. The pattern worth watching: AI agents are becoming part of the operational infrastructure at the labs building them, not just demo products. That's a meaningful feedback loop.

**Replit opens its first international office in London**

Replit CEO Amjad Masad opened the company's first office outside the US in London, with Mayor Sadiq Khan on hand for the occasion. Masad described Khan as an "AI realist" — optimistic about AI's potential but clear-eyed about the disruption coming to work, and focused on giving people skills to participate rather than just absorb the impact. Masad said that framing matches how Replit thinks about its mission.

Why it matters: Replit's pitch has always been democratizing software creation. Planting a flag in London, with a politician who's threading the "pro-AI but honest about costs" needle, is a calculated move for a company that needs regulatory goodwill in Europe while also selling to the exact workers whose jobs are changing fastest.
