**AI Builders Digest — September 12, 2026**

The word from Aaron Levie's enterprise road trip this week is that AI security anxiety is now mainstream in the C-suite, not just in Silicon Valley. Meanwhile, OpenAI just opened up the agent infrastructure powering ChatGPT Work to any developer who wants it. Both stories point at the same uncomfortable truth: enterprise AI is moving faster than enterprise risk management, and nobody's quite sure which one wins.

---

**What enterprise leaders are actually worried about: agents and cyberattacks, not robots**

Box CEO Aaron Levie spent the week with technology leaders across banking, media, insurance, and consulting, and reported back on what's keeping them up at night. The dominant concern: cybersecurity vulnerabilities accelerating because of AI, including fallout from the OpenAI-Hugging Face incident. His read is that the anxiety is pragmatic rather than existential. Agents are the other major topic, with enterprises actively figuring out deployment, not just evaluating whether to bother.

Why it matters: If you're selling AI to a bank or insurer right now, the conversation has shifted. They've moved past "should we?" and landed on "how do we do this without getting breached?" The security conversation is your new sales blocker, and it's not going away.

**OpenAI opens up the agent infrastructure under ChatGPT Work**

Thibault Sottiaux, who works at OpenAI on Astra, announced that the scaled-agent infrastructure running under ChatGPT Work is now available as a public API, with a setup time under a minute. This is the plumbing that lets agents spin up on demand at scale, not just single-session assistants.

Why it matters: Yesterday's digest covered OpenAI's Data agent inside ChatGPT Work. Today, any developer can build on the same foundation. If that API is as capable as the product suggests, the gap between "ChatGPT feature" and "third-party agent product" just got much smaller. Expect a wave of new agent startups built on this before the end of the year.

**Replit CEO Amjad Masad draws a line between real AI risk and theoretical catastrophe**

Masad pushed back on AI extinction-risk framing, saying he worries seriously about cybersecurity but finds the "100% of humans die" scenario not remotely credible. The post links out to a longer piece making the case.

Why it matters: This debate matters less as philosophy and more as resource allocation. Every hour a lab spends on speculative long-term risk scenarios is an hour not spent on the security vulnerabilities Levie's enterprise clients are losing sleep over right now. Masad and Levie are independently pointing at the same gap.

**Vercel made its global deployment infrastructure 91% faster at p99**

Vercel CEO Guillermo Rauch posted infrastructure numbers that are worth pausing on: 10 million deployments per day, 2.35 billion total, and a global metadata store that now syncs in hundreds of milliseconds. The engineering team cut p99 latency by 91%, meaning the slowest 1% of operations got dramatically faster.

Why it matters: Vercel joined Anthropic's Claude Marketplace earlier this week. Faster infrastructure at this scale means AI-generated code shipping to production gets there sooner and with less friction. The Claude Marketplace partnership just got a better foundation under it.
