**AI Builders Digest — November 5, 2026**

The agent security conversation is getting louder, and it's coming from two directions at once. Yesterday we flagged that Replit's Amjad Masad thinks agents need minimal permissions. Today, Vercel's Guillermo Rauch is shipping that instinct as a product. The gap between "good principle" and "actually deployed" is closing fast, and that's worth watching.

**Vercel's internal code security agent is now yours to use**

Guillermo Rauch, CEO of Vercel, announced that the security agent his team has been using internally, which he calls "Hacker," is now available externally. It audits every line of code automatically and works regardless of whether you host on Vercel. Rauch's pitch: you cannot rely on every engineer having security expertise, so make the audit automatic.

Why it matters: Most security tooling catches known vulnerability patterns. An agent that reasons about code context can catch the weird edge cases that pattern-matching misses. If your team ships fast and security review is a bottleneck, this is worth evaluating before your next release cycle.

https://x.com/rauchg/status/2117734833824948373

**Anthropic now auto-connects Claude to your company's plugins**

Boris Cherny from Anthropic's Claude team announced that Claude for Teams and Enterprise now handles plugin authentication automatically. Admins configure the connections once, and teammates are signed in the first time they use a plugin, with no repeated login friction.

Why it matters: Adoption of AI tools inside companies often dies on the vine because of setup friction. Every extra step between "I want to try this" and "I'm actually using this" costs you half your potential users. Making plugin access seamless removes one of the more consistent complaints from enterprise rollouts.

https://x.com/bcherny/status/2117693181651542387

**Together AI claims 3.7x faster inference with its new compilation system**

Together AI launched Together Compiler, a system that uses agents to continuously optimize how open AI models run in production. The claim: it can retune a model's inference stack in under 24 hours as traffic patterns shift, delivering up to 3.7x higher throughput compared to static setups.

Why it matters: If you're running open-source models at scale, the cost difference between well-optimized and poorly-optimized inference is not marginal. It's the difference between a viable product margin and a money-losing one. A system that adapts to your actual traffic rather than your predicted traffic could meaningfully change the economics of self-hosting.

https://www.together.ai/blog/together-compiler-continual-compilation-for-ai-inference

**DevRev is using GPT-6.1 Sol to handle enterprise customer support autonomously**

OpenAI published a case study on DevRev, which uses GPT-6.1 Sol to power its Computer and Operator products for enterprise self-service. The model was chosen specifically for its balance of reasoning capability, speed, and cost at scale.

Why it matters: GPT-6.1 Sol's mention here is notable. It's a signal that OpenAI is actively tiering its model lineup for different cost-performance tradeoffs, and enterprise service is one of the first real-world stress tests. If autonomous support can hold up at enterprise volume, the case for cutting tier-one human support headcount gets a lot harder to dismiss.

https://openai.com/index/devrev-enterprise-self-service
