**Anthropic ships Claude Sonnet 5, its most agent-ready model yet**

Anthropic released Claude Sonnet 5, billing it as their most capable model for agentic work, with particular emphasis on coding and professional tasks. The announcement is light on benchmarks so far, but "most agentic Sonnet yet" is doing real work in that description: Sonnet is the model that sits in most production deployments, not the premium Opus tier.

Why it matters: If you're running Claude in any automated workflow today, this is probably the version you'll be running next week. The shift toward agentic framing also signals where Anthropic thinks the market is going: not chatbots you talk to, but models that take actions while you're doing something else. Claude Code users especially should pay attention.

**"Just buy the tool" is no longer good enough**

Zara Zhang posted a sharp observation: buyers are losing patience with paying for software tools because, with coding agents available, they figure they can just build the same thing themselves. Her conclusion is that what people will actually pay for is expertise they genuinely can't replicate, not software wrapping that expertise.

Why it matters: If your company sells a productivity tool with a clean UI and a reasonable feature set, you may be 12 months away from a serious retention problem. The defensible businesses going forward are the ones where the value is domain knowledge, proprietary data, or a network, not the interface sitting on top of it.
