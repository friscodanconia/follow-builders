Why it matters: If you're building on OpenAI's APIs, your weekend reading is sorted. The security and enterprise tooling is where the money is, and that's usually where the boring-but-important updates hide. GPT-6 Astra will get the headlines, but check what changed in the API access and rate limit tiers first.

**Microsoft builds an AI that thinks about biology the way biology actually works**

Microsoft Research introduced Quine, a multimodal AI system built specifically for biological research. The core idea: biology doesn't organize itself into neat categories, so neither should the AI studying it. Quine connects knowledge across genes, proteins, cells, and tissues simultaneously, and it already validated several drug candidates in wet-lab experiments through a collaboration with the Broad Institute of Harvard and MIT. A Quine Fellows program will give select scientists early access.

Why it matters: Drug discovery pipelines are brutal and expensive, and most AI biology tools have been narrow, good at proteins or genomics but not both at once. If Quine can actually reason across biological scales and help researchers prioritize which compounds to test, it compresses the most expensive part of drug development. The wet-lab validation is what separates this from a demo.

https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/introducing-quine-an-ai-research-system-designed-for-the-complexity-of-biology/

**Cursor cuts its Pro plan's effective API budget in half**

Thibault Sottiaux, posting from the Cursor account, announced that the $200/month Pro subscription is reopening to new subscribers, but with a changed usage calculation that works out to roughly half the API spend of the old plan. The post promises users will still get more work done than before, attributing this to efficiency improvements rather than raw compute.

Why it matters: "You'll do more with less" is exactly what every company says when it cuts what you're paying for. If the efficiency gains are real, fine. If they're not, developers who rely on Cursor for heavy coding work will hit limits faster and have to decide whether the tool is still worth $200 a month. Watch the complaints starting to surface in developer forums over the next few weeks.

https://x.com/thsottiaux/status/2104823812042940713

**Vercel domains now searchable without login**

Vercel CEO Guillermo Rauch announced that Vercel domains can now be searched without authentication, and flagged explicitly that this is useful for AI agents browsing the web.

Why it matters: Small change, meaningful signal. Every time a major platform quietly removes an auth gate for agents specifically, the web gets a little more navigable for automated tools. Rauch knows exactly who his customers are building for.

https://x.com/rauchg/status/2104764419305796094
