**At this wealth management firm, agents prompt the humans, not the other way around**

Arca, a fintech that just raised $48.5M from General Catalyst and Index Ventures, has built an AI workflow where agents handle analysis across multi-day client processes, then surface decisions to human advisors rather than making them. Aman Singh, Arca's lead AI engineer, described the key design principle neatly: "agents prompt people more than people prompt agents." The firm built its own internal memory system that tracks every client's status, pending actions, responsible parties, and timelines across workflows that can stretch ten days. Trading and money movement stay with humans. The agents do the reading, tracking, and flagging.

Why it matters: This is the most concrete "human in the loop" implementation I've seen described in financial services, and it's not philosophical, it's structural. Arca built a custom model harness that automatically selects between Anthropic and OpenAI models based on internal benchmarks, which also means they can swap providers without rebuilding their product. If your company is designing agent workflows right now and you haven't figured out where the human handoff lives, Arca's architecture is worth studying before your compliance team figures it out for you.

**A video edit done with one (very long) prompt**

Zara Zhang posted a demo showing a coding agent completing a full video edit from a single prompt. No timeline scrubbing, no manual cuts, just a long natural-language instruction and a finished product.

Why it matters: "One prompt" is doing real work here. The detail that the prompt was very long suggests we're still in the phase where humans need to front-load all the judgment the agent can't supply. The question worth asking: is this faster than editing manually, or just differently slow?
