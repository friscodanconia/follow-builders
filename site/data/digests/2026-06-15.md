**AI Builders Digest — June 15, 2026**

Google just put $10 million on the table to solve AI's biggest coordination problem. Meanwhile, OpenAI is teaching people to work with agents, and Silicon Valley is debating whether we're judging AI models by the wrong metrics entirely.

**Google bets $10M that multi-agent AI will break before it breaks us**
Google DeepMind announced a $10 million funding call for multi-agent safety research. The focus is on what happens when multiple AI systems interact, coordinate, or compete with each other. Think less "will one AI go rogue" and more "what happens when a thousand AIs negotiate with each other faster than humans can follow."
Why it matters: This is Google admitting that the future isn't one superintelligent AI, but swarms of specialized agents that need to work together safely. The company that figures out multi-agent coordination first controls the next phase of AI deployment.
https://deepmind.google/blog/investing-in-multi-agent-ai-safety-research/

**OpenAI launches Academy courses for the agent era**
OpenAI introduced three new Academy courses designed to teach people practical AI skills, repeatable workflows, and how to apply agents in everyday work. This isn't technical training for developers, it's "AI literacy for everyone" focused on real workplace scenarios.
Why it matters: OpenAI is positioning itself as the adult education provider for the AI transition. When your company mandates AI training next year, there's a good chance employees will be taking OpenAI's courses, not your internal workshops.
https://openai.com/index/academy-courses-applying-ai-at-work

**Box CEO Aaron Levie calls AI jailbreaking research "standard," not conspiracy**
Box CEO Aaron Levie responded to speculation about AI model testing, noting that jailbreaking attempts are standard research practice. He argued that sharing such research with government agencies is "the whole point" and dismissed conspiracy theories about the process.
Why it matters: As AI models get more powerful, every security test becomes a political flashpoint. Levie's trying to normalize what should be routine safety research before public hysteria makes it impossible to do properly.
https://x.com/levie/status/2065964446489710939

**Y Combinator's Garry Tan wishes people would actually use AI before judging it**
Y Combinator CEO Garry Tan posted that he wishes "the significance of the model came from more people actually using it" rather than learning about AI through media coverage and hype cycles. He noted that most people form opinions based on "signifiers" rather than direct experience.
Why it matters: This is the venture capital equivalent of "try it before you buy it." If the person funding AI startups thinks public perception is disconnected from reality, expect a wave of companies focused on hands-on demos rather than technical benchmarks.
https://x.com/garrytan/status/2065791421362352476
