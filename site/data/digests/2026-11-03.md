**A legal AI startup hit $100M ARR in 18 months. Here's what they learned building agents that can't fail.**

Legora CTO Jacob Lauritzen joined the Latent Space podcast after his company closed back-to-back $550M rounds this year, at $5.55B in March and $8.8B in October. The company started in a Stockholm basement with ChatGPT, Swedish law, and a search bar. It now drafts and edits Word documents, reviews thousands of contracts simultaneously, and coordinates work across lawyers, clients, and other agents. The most interesting part of the conversation is about evaluation: how do you test AI agents when you can't look at your customers' confidential legal files to see where the agent went wrong?

Why it matters: Every company building agents for regulated industries (legal, finance, healthcare) has this problem. Your QA process assumes you can read the inputs. In legal work, you often can't. Legora's workaround to that constraint is worth studying before you hit it yourself at a much smaller scale.

https://latent.space/p/building-ai-for-lawyers-jacob-lauritzen-cto-of-legora/

**OpenAI built a custom version of Git because Codex broke the original**

OpenAI published a technical post about Pando, a Git-compatible backend the company built internally because standard Git tooling couldn't handle the volume of code changes that Codex (its AI coding agent) generates at scale. The post walks through the architectural decisions behind making version control work when AI is doing the committing at a rate no human team ever would.

Why it matters: This is a preview of an infrastructure problem your engineering team will hit when AI coding agents go from "assistant" to "contributor." Git was built for humans pushing code a few times a day. Agents push code continuously. The tooling hasn't caught up, and OpenAI just showed how ugly it gets when you hit that wall.

https://openai.com/index/scaling-git-at-openai

**Replit turns user complaints into agent build instructions automatically**

Replit CEO Amjad Masad flagged that Replit now has a feedback widget you can add to any site you've built on the platform. When users submit feedback, it gets routed directly to an AI agent as a build prompt, so complaints and feature requests become code changes without a human developer in the middle.

Why it matters: The handoff from "user said something is broken" to "developer prioritized and fixed it" is where most small products leak. Collapsing that loop into a single toggle is a real workflow change for solo builders and small teams, not just a demo.

https://x.com/amasad/status/2116998155925360965

**Google DeepMind is helping the UK government cut planning assessment times**

Google DeepMind published details of a collaboration with the UK government to speed up planning decisions, the bureaucratic process local councils use to approve or reject development projects. AI is being applied to assess planning applications faster.

Why it matters: Government procurement moves slowly and this announcement is light on specifics, so treat this as early signal rather than shipped product. But if the model works, every country with a planning backlog (which is most of them) becomes a potential market. UK housing policy is famously gridlocked, and even modest acceleration would have real economic effects.

https://deepmind.google/blog/speeding-up-planning-decisions-with-ai/
