**OpenAI's own employees went from dabbling to all-in on AI, and the numbers are striking**

Latent Space flagged new data from OpenAI's internal economic research: median output token usage among active internal users grew 56x in Research, 32x in Customer Support, and 27x in Engineering since November 2025. Legal, often the last department to touch anything new, still grew 13x. This wasn't caused by new access or lifted quotas. OpenAI employees have had unlimited access the whole time. They just weren't using it like this until recently.

Why it matters: If the people who built Claude and GPT-4 and Codex were still dramatically underusing AI six months ago, your company's "we've adopted AI" memo probably means less than you think. The unlock isn't access or even training. Something about the tools themselves crossed a threshold that changed daily behavior. The question worth asking your team: what changed for you in the last six months, and if the answer is "nothing," that's your answer.

**Guillermo Rauch sees AI triggering a wave of small business creation**

Vercel CEO Guillermo Rauch posted that AI will produce an unprecedented surge in entrepreneurship, from solo founders to revitalized small and medium businesses to the largest companies of the era. Short post, but 475 likes and 79 replies suggests it touched a nerve.

Why it matters: The interesting tension here is that most AI productivity conversation focuses on large enterprises cutting headcount. Rauch is pointing the other direction: the same tools that let a 500-person company do more with fewer people also let one person do what used to require five. If he's right, the real economic disruption isn't layoffs at big companies, it's a Cambrian explosion of small ones that never needed to hire in the first place.

**Google Labs wins Cannes Lions Grand Prix for AI Craft with Project Genie**

Google Labs announced that Project Genie took the top prize at Cannes Lions in the AI Craft category. The award comes from the advertising world's most prestigious festival, not a tech conference.

Why it matters: Creative awards don't usually move markets, but this one signals something worth watching. When adland's biggest show creates an "AI Craft" category and hands the top prize to a Google project, it means the creative industry has moved from debating whether to use AI to competing on how well they use it. Agencies still arguing about AI ethics in internal Slack channels are now a full award cycle behind.

**Swyx's conference talk tips for AI engineers**

Swyx shared prep advice for the AI Engineer Summit talks happening next week. The sharpest rules: cap AI-generated images at four per deck, put actual code on screen because engineers want to see it, and be pointy with one strong message rather than five weak ones.

Why it matters: This is practical enough to save someone from a bad talk next week, but the underlying point generalizes. The AI conference circuit has filled up with slides full of AI-generated imagery and vague claims. The advice to be "pointy" with concrete examples is the right correction.
