**AI Builders Digest — November 8, 2026**

Today's payload is thin on announcements and heavy on signals. Read together, three of these items sketch the same underlying idea: the best AI products are the ones that disappear into tools people already use, and the best AI workflows are the ones that shrink as models get smarter. That's a different vision than "build a new app for AI." It's also a harder one to monetize.

**Creator apps are quietly becoming the most interesting AI delivery mechanism**

Thariq, a builder whose work sits at the intersection of agents and product design, flagged a pattern worth watching: apps like Instagram Edits and Roblox Studio are shipping with built-in MCP (model context protocol) support, letting AI agents handle parts of the work while users pick up the rest in the tools they already know. This is not a new AI app. It is AI wired into the place the user already lives.

Why it matters: Every enterprise that built a standalone AI portal in 2025 is now competing with this model. When Roblox ships AI assistance directly into its studio and Instagram does the same in Edits, the bar for "why should I open a separate AI tool" gets much higher. If your product requires a tab switch to use AI, that is now a liability.

**Y Combinator's Garry Tan deleted a feature because the model got good enough to make it redundant**

Garry Tan shared a small but telling detail from his own agent-building work: he removed a custom "/investigate" skill from his agent because Anthropic's Opus 5.5 now handles the task better natively. His broader point is that eval-driven development loops are necessary precisely because models keep improving and what your scaffolding needs to do keeps shrinking.

Why it matters: Most teams building on top of AI models are accumulating custom logic, not shedding it. Tan's approach runs the other way: build it, test it, and delete it when the base model catches up. If you are not running evals regularly, you will not know when to delete things, and you will end up maintaining infrastructure that is making your product worse than the raw model.
