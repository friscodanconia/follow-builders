**AI Builders Digest — June 25, 2026**

Two things happened this week that fit together better than either company planned: Anthropic shipped Claude Tag into Slack, and OpenAI announced its first custom chip. One company is going deeper into where work actually happens. The other is going deeper into the stack that makes it possible. The interesting question is which bet pays off faster.

---

**OpenAI built its own chip, and the 9-month timeline is the real story**

OpenAI announced Jalapeño, its first custom AI chip for inference, designed with Broadcom and intended to handle ChatGPT, Codex, API traffic, and future agent products. The chip itself is a standard "own more of the stack" move. What's notable is the reported 9-month design-to-tapeout cycle, which is unusually fast for a high-performance chip and reportedly accelerated by OpenAI's own models helping with the design. Latent Space's AI News also covers the broader "meta-harness" moment happening in parallel: a wave of open architectures for coordinating AI agents, with Databricks CTO Matei Zaharia's Omnigent project as the latest entrant.

Why it matters: If AI models can meaningfully compress chip design timelines, the cost and speed of building custom silicon changes for anyone who can afford to try. That's a small club today. It won't be in three years.

**Anthropic brings Claude into your Slack, with permission controls**

Claude Tag launched in beta for Slack on Enterprise and Team plans. The feature lets teams summon Claude directly in Slack channels, and Anthropic shipped a configuration guide for setting agent permissions, meaning admins can control what Claude is allowed to do when tagged.

Why it matters: The permissions piece is what separates this from a gimmick. Every enterprise that's been nervous about an AI reading the wrong channel or taking unintended actions now has a documented control surface. That removes one of the standard objections from IT and security teams who've been blocking these rollouts.

**Vercel's Guillermo Rauch wants your sharpest complaints about Eve**

Vercel CEO Guillermo Rauch posted that he's building a private X Chat group for developers with "high quality critical feedback" on Eve, Vercel's agent harness product. He's offering direct access to Eve engineers in exchange for serious, detailed input.

Why it matters: This is a smarter way to do product feedback than a public forum, and it signals Eve is still being shaped rather than finalized. If you're building agents on Vercel's stack and have specific friction points, this is a rare window to actually influence the roadmap.

**Google Gemini adds a World Cup image template**

Google Gemini Labs VP Josh Woodward posted a soccer-themed feature: users can turn a photo into a custom trading card, mural, or plushie using a new template in the Gemini app. Select "Get in the game," upload a photo, done.

A World Cup marketing push, clearly, but the template-based image generation flow is worth a look if you're building consumer products around image personalization.
