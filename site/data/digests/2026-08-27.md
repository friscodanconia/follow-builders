**Your AI benchmark is probably already out of date**

Madhu Guru posted part 9 of his ongoing series on building better evals, focused on what he calls "the eval roadmap problem." The core argument: most teams build their benchmarks once and then leave them static, while their users keep asking harder questions. He uses a financial research agent as the example. Week one, users ask it to summarize a five-page earnings report. Two months later, they're asking it to compare five years of earnings across competitors. The same eval that passed the agent in week one tells you nothing about whether it's good enough in month two.

Why it matters: Yesterday's installment covered discriminatory power, the problem of evals where every model scores between 92 and 95 and you learn nothing. This one is the logical follow-on: even if your eval was well-designed at launch, users evolve and evals don't. If your team shipped an AI feature six months ago and hasn't updated the benchmark since, you are flying blind on whether it still works for what your users are actually doing.
