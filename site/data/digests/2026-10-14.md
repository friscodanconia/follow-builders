**Google's new flagship model promises to solve your hardest problems**

Google announced Gemini 4 Argon yesterday, positioning it as a frontier model for developers, enterprises, and scientists. The blog post is thin on specifics: no benchmark numbers, no pricing details, no head-to-head comparisons published yet. What's there is the framing: "your hardest problems," which is doing a lot of work.

Why it matters: Google has a pattern of announcing frontier models and then spending the next quarter explaining what they actually do. If Argon's launch post is this sparse, the real story will be the developer docs and API pricing, which tell you who Google actually built this for. Watch the Gemini API pricing page before you update your roadmap.

**A robot folded napkins for 24 hours straight. Here's how.**

Dyna Robotics co-founder Jason Ma sat down with Latent Space's Swyx at AI Engineer NYC to walk through how Dyna-2.1 achieves 99.4% success rates on long, uninterrupted tasks in real commercial kitchens. The trick: a world-action model pre-trained on one million hours of human video, combined with what Dyna calls "agentic harnesses" that let robots execute hour-long workflows without a human reset. The robot already folds thousands of napkins daily at Din Tai Fung. New tasks can be learned from 45 minutes of demonstrations.

Why it matters: The graveyard of robotics startups is full of impressive demos. What separates Dyna is the specificity of the deployment: a named restaurant, a measurable success rate, a production volume. The conversation also covers why dexterous hands and missing sensory data are still major bottlenecks. If you're evaluating robotics vendors, the gap between "demo-ready" and "Din Tai Fung-ready" is the question to ask.

https://www.latent.space/p/dyna

**Higgsfield can now make AI videos longer than 10 minutes**

Higgsfield, a video creation tool built on OpenAI's models, extended its maximum video length from roughly one minute to over ten, and added voice-directed filmmaking via GPT-Live. The technical stack is GPT-6 Astra for video, GPT-Image-2.5 for precise visuals.

Why it matters: Ten-minute AI video is the threshold where short-form content tools start competing with actual production workflows. Marketing teams that currently use Higgsfield for social clips will start running into editorial teams asking why they need a video crew.

https://openai.com/index/higgsfield-longer-videos-voice

**Box CEO Aaron Levie: cybersecurity is where agent swarms earn their keep**

Box CEO Aaron Levie argues that agent swarms, groups of AI agents running in parallel on the same problem, will become standard in enterprise workflows, and that cybersecurity is the clearest early use case. His reasoning: security work requires testing vast numbers of attack permutations without fatigue, and agents can chain together steps across a wide risk-vector search space in a way no human team can match at scale.

Why it matters: Your security team is already understaffed. The first enterprise software vendor that ships a credible agent-swarm penetration testing product at a fraction of consultant rates is going to close a lot of deals fast. Levie is pointing at the category before the product exists at scale, which is usually worth paying attention to.
