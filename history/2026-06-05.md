**AI Builders Digest — June 5, 2026**

Yesterday we covered the no-code versus coding agent debate. Today, we're seeing what happens when established SaaS companies start building their counter-attack.

**Figma's director makes the case against AI killing SaaS**
Dan Shipper interviewed Figma's director of product management for developers, who argued that AI agents actually make people more willing to pay for specialized SaaS tools, not less. The key insight: chat-based interfaces are fundamentally wrong for design work. Figma is betting on their new MCP server that lets you work with live web pages and approach design from multiple directions simultaneously.
Why it matters: If Figma can prove that professional tools with purpose-built interfaces beat general-purpose AI chat, it's a roadmap for every SaaS company trying to survive the agent revolution.

**OpenAI showcases how Endava rebuilt software delivery around agents**
OpenAI published a case study on how consulting firm Endava is using AI agents, ChatGPT Enterprise, and Codex to completely redesign their software development process. The focus is on automating workflows and building what they call an "AI-native culture" across the entire enterprise.
Why it matters: This is OpenAI's blueprint for how traditional consulting firms should reorganize around AI. When a 12,000-person company rebuilds their core service delivery, the playbook matters for everyone downstream.
https://openai.com/index/endava-frontiers

**Google Labs launches Dreambeans: "hope scrolling, not doom scrolling"**
Google product leader Josh Woodward announced the launch of Dreambeans, a new app from Google Labs designed to deliver daily inspiration without the endless scroll addiction. The internal pitch was literally "hope scrolling, not doom scrolling." It's rolling out today as an experiment from a small Labs team.
Why it matters: Google is quietly testing whether AI can fix social media's attention economy problems. If Dreambeans works, expect every platform to copy the approach.
