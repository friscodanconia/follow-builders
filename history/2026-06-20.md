**Claude Code can now publish living documents to your whole team**

Anthropic shipped Artifacts inside Claude Code for Team and Enterprise users: interactive HTML pages built directly from your coding session, shared with teammates at a private link. Think PR walkthroughs, project dashboards, or architecture diagrams that update as your work evolves, not static screenshots pasted into Slack. It's in beta, and Cat Wu, who works on Claude Code at Anthropic, says it has already changed how their internal team communicates.

Why it matters: The fastest way to slow down a team isn't bad code, it's bad communication about the code. If Claude can turn a coding session into a shareable, interactive brief without anyone writing docs, the "AI saves developer time" claim starts having a concrete answer for skeptical engineering managers.

**Bernie Sanders introduced a bill to claim 50% ownership of any AI startup crossing $200M in revenue**

Y Combinator president Garry Tan flagged the legislation, framing it as part of a pattern: the same legislative coalition that tried to ban startup acquisitions now wants a government equity stake in any AI company that reaches real scale. Combined, these measures would eliminate both the exit most founders count on (acquisition) and the upside that makes the earlier risk worth taking.

Why it matters: If this bill passed, the expected value of building an AI startup in the United States drops overnight. Founders who are currently choosing between building in San Francisco or Singapore just got a new data point for that spreadsheet.

**The one-shot AI agent is a trap. Here's what works instead.**

Nan Yu, a builder in the AI productivity space, shared a hard-won lesson from shipping an agent that writes project updates: the first version let the AI produce the whole thing without asking the user anything. The output was polished-sounding nonsense, because users stopped thinking and just accepted whatever came out. Switching to a multi-turn interactive mode, where the agent asks what to emphasize and what context is missing, produced dramatically better results.

Why it matters: Every team currently building "autonomous" agents that fire off a result with no back-and-forth is likely producing the same invisible slop. The users look satisfied until someone reads the output carefully. Building in friction on purpose is counterintuitive, and it's probably the right call.
