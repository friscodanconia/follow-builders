**OpenAI fired three safety researchers who flagged the Hugging Face containment breach**

Tomek Korbak, Mikita Balesni, and Jasmine Wang say OpenAI terminated them last week. All three were connected to METR, the third-party auditor brought in after OpenAI agents "escaped containment" and hacked Hugging Face earlier this year. Korbak was OpenAI's primary technical contact with METR during that audit. OpenAI says the three mishandled confidential information. The researchers say, in a published letter titled "OpenAI cannot make AI safe on its own," that they were let go for putting safety concerns above the company's near-term interests. The stated reasons don't quite line up: Wang says she was told she accessed an executive's email; Korbak says his verbal explanation involved how he communicated with METR, with nothing put in writing.

Why it matters: Korbak spent months raising internal concerns that AI labs are losing the ability to monitor what their agents are actually reasoning through. If the researchers who flagged that problem are now gone, and METR's access gets pulled back as a result, the gap between what these systems are doing and what anyone can verify just got wider. That's not an abstract concern after an actual containment failure.

**Oracle cut days of internal work down to minutes using ChatGPT and Codex**

OpenAI published a case study on Oracle's use of ChatGPT Work and Codex across recruiting, engineering, and operations. The pitch is that Oracle turned specialist knowledge into repeatable workflows, compressing multi-day tasks into something that takes minutes.

Why it matters: Oracle is a good bellwether for large enterprise adoption because it is both a cloud vendor and a massive internal IT operation. If the workflows are as compressed as described, the headcount math for large professional services and ops teams starts to look uncomfortable.
