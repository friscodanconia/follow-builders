**YC's Garry Tan downloads million-token coding agents to his MacBook**
Y Combinator CEO Garry Tan shared his excitement about running a coding agent with a 1-million-token context window locally on a 128GB MacBook Pro. The model claims "usable coding agent capability" and can process massive amounts of code without sending anything to the cloud.
Why it matters: When one of Silicon Valley's most influential startup backers can run enterprise-grade AI on his laptop, every company paying thousands monthly for cloud AI inference is about to recalculate their costs. Local AI just became a serious threat to the cloud giants' revenue model.

**OpenAI explains how it keeps Codex from breaking things**
OpenAI published a detailed post on running its Codex coding system safely in production environments. The company uses sandboxing, approval workflows, network policies, and "agent-native telemetry" to prevent coding agents from accessing sensitive systems or making unauthorized changes.
Why it matters: Every company about to deploy coding agents is asking the same question: how do we stop them from accidentally deleting the production database? OpenAI just published the playbook, which means they expect a lot more companies to need it soon.

Anthropic's Alex Albert revealed that Claude Mythos Preview, an unreleased model snapshot, scored more than double the "time horizon" of the next-best model on METR's 80% success rate benchmark. The benchmark tests how far ahead AI models can plan and execute multi-step tasks.
Why it matters: Planning ahead is the difference between AI that follows instructions and AI that can actually manage projects. If Claude can think twice as far into the future as GPT-4 or Gemini, that's not just a better model, that's a different category of capability.

**Developer ditches markdown for AI-generated HTML**

Why it matters: This is a canary in the coal mine for technical writing tools. When developers trust AI enough to generate their documentation format, every company selling markdown editors and static site generators should be worried.
