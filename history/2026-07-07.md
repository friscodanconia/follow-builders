**Anthropic's new Claude Sonnet 5 is built for agents, not just chat**

Anthropic released Claude Sonnet 5, describing it as their most "agentic" Sonnet yet, with particular focus on coding and professional work. The previous Sonnet models have been the workhorse tier, cheaper than Opus but capable enough for most production use cases. This release pushes that middle tier further into autonomous task territory.

Why it matters: Most companies deploying Claude in production aren't using the flagship model. They're using Sonnet because it's faster and cheaper. If Sonnet 5 is meaningfully better at multi-step autonomous tasks, your costs stay roughly the same while the AI does more of the work without hand-holding. That's the upgrade that actually moves the needle on ROI.

**Y Combinator's Garry Tan on why AI deleted the bottleneck**

Garry Tan, president of Y Combinator, posted his framing of the current moment: the real constraint on wealth was never resources but ideas and the leverage to act on them. His argument is that AI eliminated the leverage problem, leaving only the ideas themselves.

Why it matters: This is the motivational-posting version of a claim worth taking seriously. If execution leverage is no longer scarce, the founders who win won't be the ones who can build fastest. They'll be the ones who figured out what to build. Your competitive advantage just shifted from engineering speed to judgment.
