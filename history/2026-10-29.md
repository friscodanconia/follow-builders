**OpenAI and ServiceNow go deep on enterprise workflows**

OpenAI and ServiceNow announced an expanded partnership that embeds OpenAI models and agents directly into ServiceNow's platform, while pulling ServiceNow workflows into ChatGPT and Codex. If your company runs ServiceNow for IT ticketing, HR requests, or operations, this is the plumbing that would let an AI agent actually do something when it reads a ticket, not just summarize it.

Why it matters: ServiceNow runs the back office for a huge slice of the Fortune 500. This partnership means the bottleneck for AI agents in large companies just got shorter. Your IT department's "submit a ticket" workflow is about to look very different, and the humans who triaged those tickets are about to have a harder conversation with their managers.

**Why AI agents keep picking Supabase as their default backend**

Latent Space's Swyx ran a benchmark of frontier AI models building apps from scratch and found that Supabase, the open-source database platform, won as the first-choice backend across nearly every model tested. Supabase founders Paul Copplestone and Ant Wilson joined the podcast to explain what's behind that. The short version: agents don't just encounter database setup friction once, they hit it repeatedly as they create and test environments. Supabase's new local runtime (no Docker required), declarative schema files, and compute-beside-database design each remove a piece of that repeated friction. The company went from YC S20 to a $10 billion valuation building on Postgres, something that shouldn't have worked this well this fast.

Why it matters: The developer tools that win the agent era won't necessarily be the most powerful ones. They'll be the ones that reduce the number of steps an agent has to take to get to a working environment. If you're building on a backend that requires a lot of setup ceremony, your agents are paying a tax on every run. That adds up.

https://latent.space/p/supabase/
