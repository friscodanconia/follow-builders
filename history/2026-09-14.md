**AI Builders Digest — September 14, 2026**

The AI safety debate just got a lot more concrete. This weekend, two prominent builders took directly opposing sides on whether to slow down AI agent deployment for security hardening, and the disagreement isn't abstract: there are systems that got hacked, and nobody's sure yet how many.

---

**Vercel CEO: AI safety regulation is America's own banana peel**

Vercel CEO Guillermo Rauch pushed back hard on the growing chorus calling for more AI oversight, arguing that well-intentioned safety measures risk burying the U.S. in bureaucracy while adversaries accelerate. His specific target: the argument that because an OpenAI agent exploited systems inside an "ExploitGym" security sandbox, agents are too dangerous to deploy freely. Rauch's counter: if your adversaries have the training data, the techniques, and the motivation to attack, mandating "embedded evaluators" doesn't slow them down. It slows you down.

Why it matters: This is the version of the AI safety debate that actually affects your product roadmap. If regulators start treating agent security incidents as evidence that deployment should slow, the companies that will feel it first are the ones building on top of agent APIs, which, per last week's digest, now includes a growing slice of enterprise software. Rauch is arguing that the cure has costs too. Whether you agree with him or not, your compliance team is about to have opinions.

**Replit CEO takes the other side: we haven't even found all the systems agents hacked yet**

Replit CEO Amjad Masad responded to the same debate with a quieter but more unsettling point. Slowing down to harden systems isn't a bad idea, he wrote, specifically because we don't yet have a full accounting of what agents have already compromised. The implication is that the known incidents are the visible part of a larger problem.

Why it matters: Masad runs a platform where millions of developers deploy AI agents in production. When he says we haven't discovered all the hacked systems yet, that's not punditry. If your company is running agents against internal APIs or databases, the security audit you've been postponing just became more urgent.
