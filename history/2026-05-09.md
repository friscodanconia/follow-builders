**AI Builders Digest — May 9, 2026**

Anthropic is now worth more than most Fortune 500 companies while traditional tech giants are cutting staff to "prepare for AI." The math is simple: companies that build AI are printing money, companies that use AI are firing people, and nobody wants to admit what that means for the middle.

**Firefox team fixes 15 months of security bugs in one month with Claude**
Anthropic's Alex Albert shared that Mozilla's Firefox team, using Claude Mythos Preview, fixed more security bugs in April alone than they had in the previous 15 months combined. No details on the specific process, but the numbers speak for themselves.
Why it matters: Security bugs are some of the hardest problems in software — they require deep code analysis and pattern recognition across massive codebases. If AI can tackle those at 15x speed, every software company's security team is about to get very productive or very small.
https://x.com/alexalbert__/status/2052468573516513762

**Box CEO Aaron Levie warns about the AI productivity trap**
Levie argues that when AI makes something easier for you, it makes it easier for everyone else too. "If AI makes building software easier, then there will be a relative increase in resources going into sales, marketing, and customer success, because standing out becomes even more important." Competitive forces will push resources toward whatever AI can't automate.
Why it matters: Your company's plan to cut engineering costs with AI agents is going to work. So is your competitor's. The advantage goes to whoever reinvests those savings into the parts of business that still require humans — like convincing customers to pick you over the other guy with identical AI-built software.
https://x.com/levie/status/2052566788236509254

**Anthropic hits $1.2 trillion valuation while others lay off workers**
Latent Space reports that Anthropic's secondary market valuation has reached $1-1.2 trillion, making it more valuable than OpenAI and placing it among the world's 15 most valuable companies. Meanwhile, Block cut 40% of staff, Coinbase 14%, and Cloudflare 20%, all citing "AI readiness." The contrast is stark: AI builders are growing 10x annually while everyone else shrinks.
Why it matters: We're watching the economy split in real time. Companies that build AI are becoming some of the most valuable entities in human history. Companies that buy AI are using it as cover to cut costs. The middle ground is disappearing fast.
https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-anthropic-growing-10xyear

**Y Combinator's Garry Tan releases browser control setup for Claude**
Tan shared "GStack," his personal setup that lets Claude Code and other AI agents control web browsers with full login access. The system runs on Mac/PC and uses a "/pair-agent" command to give AI models direct browser control through MCP (Model Control Protocol).
Why it matters: When prominent investors start sharing tools for AI agents to browse the web with their actual login credentials, we've crossed into "AI agents as employees" territory. Your next hire might not need a laptop — just browser access.
https://x.com/garrytan/status/2052588782076281324

**OpenAI details how it runs coding agents safely**
OpenAI published a technical overview of how it securely operates Codex, its coding agent platform. The system uses sandboxing, approval workflows, network policies, and "agent-native telemetry" to prevent AI from accidentally breaking things or accessing restricted systems.
Why it matters: OpenAI is essentially publishing the playbook for running AI agents in enterprise environments without getting fired when they inevitably do something unexpected. Every company deploying coding agents will copy this approach.
