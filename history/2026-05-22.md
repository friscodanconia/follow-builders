**AI Builders Digest — May 22, 2026**

Yesterday we covered CIOs panicking about token costs. Today we see why: the enterprise AI market just consolidated around three companies, and the job roles inside companies are getting completely rewritten.

**Anthropic acquires Stainless API — whose biggest customers were OpenAI and Google**

AI newsletter editor Dan Shipper revealed that Anthropic just bought developer tool startup Stainless API, a company that specialized in building APIs, SDKs, and MCP servers for AI systems. The irony: Stainless's biggest customers were OpenAI and Google, Anthropic's main competitors. Shipper had interviewed Stainless CEO Alex Rattray last October about the "unglamorous plumbing that makes AI agents actually work."

Why it matters: Anthropic just bought the company that was building the infrastructure for their competitors' AI agents. This is either brilliant strategic thinking or about to create some very awkward client calls for the Stainless team.

**The new org chart for AI-first companies**

Zara Zhang laid out how job roles need to flip in AI-native teams: individual contributors should start thinking like managers (delegating tasks to AI agents, setting standards, verifying output), while managers should start thinking like individual contributors (being more hands-on builders instead of just doing people management).

Why it matters: Your company's hierarchy is about to invert. The best individual contributors will be managing a team of AI agents, while their managers will be coding again. HR departments everywhere are scrambling to figure out how to evaluate and compensate this.

**Google's Project Genie now available to all AI Ultra subscribers**

Google Labs announced that Project Genie, their tool for creating interactive game worlds, is now fully available to all Google AI Ultra subscribers globally (18+). The company highlighted user enthusiasm for the worlds people have been creating.

Why it matters: Google is betting that AI-generated interactive content will be the killer app that justifies a $30/month subscription. If your kids start asking for Google AI Ultra to build games instead of asking for Roblox, that bet paid off.

**Exa crushes search API competition in 90-minute bakeoff**

Developer advocate Swyx reported that his team did a competitive evaluation of search APIs and unanimously chose Exa over all competitors in just 1.5 hours. He noted his pride in seeing his "former landlords" (presumably former roommates who founded Exa) succeed.

Why it matters: When technical teams can pick a clear winner in 90 minutes, that's not a close competition. Exa is apparently solving the "AI needs better search" problem well enough that evaluations are becoming formalities.

**Box CEO explains why AI deployment is nothing like cloud adoption**

Box CEO Aaron Levie highlighted a post about Field Deployment Engineers (FDEs) and explained why AI adoption is fundamentally different from previous technology shifts like cloud computing. Unlike cloud, which mainly affected developers and IT teams, AI requires "fundamental changes to the workflows of employees" across entire organizations.

Why it matters: Every company hiring "AI transformation consultants" needs to understand this distinction. You're not just switching technology stacks. You're rewiring how every employee works. That's why FDEs who can manage this transition are about to become the highest-paid people in enterprise tech.
