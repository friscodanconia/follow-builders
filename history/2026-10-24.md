**AI Builders Digest — October 24, 2026**

Security is this week's through-line, and it's pointing in two directions at once. Aaron Levie says AI agents will tear through every legacy system that relied on secrecy to stay safe. Boris Cherny at Anthropic just handed the industry a shared standard for what those agents can actually do. Both moves matter more together than they do apart.

---

**Anthropic donates Agent Skills to an open foundation**

Boris Cherny, who works on Claude Code at Anthropic, announced that Agent Skills, a framework that started as an internal experiment, has been donated to the Agentic AI Foundation alongside MCP. The decision came because enough of the industry had already adopted it that keeping it inside Anthropic started to look like the wrong call.

Why it matters: MCP gave AI agents a common way to talk to tools. Agent Skills appears to be doing the same thing for what agents are actually capable of declaring and sharing. When Anthropic donates something it built to a neutral foundation rather than keeping it proprietary, that's a signal competitors will build on it too. Watch for this to show up in products you use well before anyone announces it.

**Aaron Levie: security through obscurity is done, and the rebuild starts now**

Box CEO Aaron Levie, making his fourth appearance in this digest this week, made a sharp argument: AI agents that can try every attack permutation automatically have killed "security through obscurity" as a viable strategy. His conclusion is that years of legacy enterprise infrastructure now needs to be rebuilt from the ground up. Access controls, data pipelines, the works.

Why it matters: If your company's security posture relies on anything being hard to find rather than genuinely protected, that assumption is already wrong. The same agents your IT team is buying to automate work are the same class of system attackers are running against your network. The rebuild Levie is describing is expensive, slow, and not optional.

**Swyx on AI-found bugs: fix them, don't fight the messenger**

Latent Space co-founder Swyx weighed in on what appears to be a controversy about AI systems surfacing software bugs at an uncomfortable rate. His take: stop complaining that AI found them and start closing them.

Why it matters: There's a version of this conversation happening at every engineering team right now. AI code review tools are finding problems humans missed for years, and the instinct in some orgs is to question the tool rather than the codebase. Swyx's framing is the right one, even if the reply count suggests not everyone agrees.
