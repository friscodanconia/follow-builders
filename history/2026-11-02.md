**Korea's $1 trillion market crash is a stress test for the whole AI supply chain**

Richard Gray, an analyst at a Washington foreign policy think tank, walks through why South Korea's stock market swung from world's best performer to shedding over $1 trillion in the span of roughly a year. The short version: Korea's market is functionally a leveraged bet on two companies, Samsung and SK hynix, which together control most of the world's supply of high-bandwidth memory chips, the specific type of memory that AI data centers run on. When AI spending surges, Korea soars. When Chinese competitors close the gap, or when any doubt creeps into AI capex projections, Korea craters. The June-July 2026 crash sent ripples well beyond Seoul.

Why it matters: If you work at a company that buys GPUs, the memory those chips use comes almost entirely from two suppliers in one country with shallow capital markets and a new Chinese competitor eating into their margins. That's not a supply chain, that's a single point of failure. The next time an AI spending cycle turns, expect the Korean market to be the canary.

**Box CEO Aaron Levie on when AI stops being a speed boost and starts being a capability jump**

Aaron Levie posted a take worth sitting with: until about six months ago, the best AI could do was help you produce what you already would have produced, just faster. He argues we've crossed into different territory, where the output of working with AI should now dramatically exceed what was previously possible, not just arrive sooner. His litmus test: if your AI-assisted prototype isn't fully functional and interactive in a way that would have been out of reach before, you're not pushing hard enough.

Why it matters: Most companies are still measuring AI by time saved on existing tasks. Levie's framing suggests that's the wrong metric entirely, and companies still optimizing for "10% faster" are going to look very slow next to teams asking "what can we build that we literally could not have built before."

**Swyx makes a case for "low effort plus critique" over maxing out AI settings**

Swyx, writer and AI researcher behind the Latent Space podcast, pushed back on the instinct to crank every AI model to its highest settings. His argument: a loop of low-effort drafts followed by structured critique beats burning maximum compute on a single high-effort generation, especially when you're throughput-constrained. He linked to a resource on his site about babysitting long AI runs as supporting context.

Why it matters: Most teams default to "use the best model at max settings." Swyx is pointing at a workflow pattern that's cheaper and potentially faster for iterative work. If your team is running up against API rate limits or costs, this loop deserves a look before you just upgrade your tier.
